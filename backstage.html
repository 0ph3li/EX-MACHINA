A SHORT HISTORY OF THE CRANE

431 BC

img/BACKSTAGE/crane.jpg · cables hanging from a ceiling, a room full of them

The mēchanē

athens. euripides ends medea with a chariot hoisted over the stage on ropes and a wooden arm. the plot was stuck; the machine pulled it out. the ropes are cables now. there are a lot more of them.

19 BC

img/BACKSTAGE/horace.jpg · a veiled marble face, a crown of flowers

“Nec deus intersit”

rome. horace writes the rule: don’t bring a god on stage unless the knot is worthy of one. back then the gods were marble, veiled, and very patient. most bugs don’t deserve one. some do.

the first bug

1947

img/BACKSTAGE/moth.jpg · a moth, wings open, on black

The moth

harvard, the mark ii. a moth flies into a relay and stops the machine. they tape it into the logbook: “first actual case of bug being found.” the year was forty-seven. so is the line. it’s always 47.

2026

img/BACKSTAGE/stage.jpg · a screen: “we’ve started over (again)”

Starting over (again)

ex-machina. the crane is software now, and it doesn’t mind starting over. it reads the whole repo, finds the line, writes the ending, and this time it gets it right. then it leaves before the bow.
